### D-100 · 2026-08-15 · Accepted — ground cover is cosmetic, client-derived, and not fog-gated

**Decision:** The bare ground between D-087's forests grows small,
region-specific foliage. Five parts:

1. **Eighteen authored props**, 24–72 triangles each, generated by committed
   Python (`art/scatter/props.py`) on the existing `art/lib/geom.py` and baked
   to `generated/models/prop_*.glb` by `just build-assets` — ferns, tufts,
   wildflowers, shrubs, mushrooms, fallen logs, mossy rocks, boulders, dry
   tussocks, cacti, cracked slabs, reeds, dune grass, driftwood, shells,
   scree, rock spikes, snow-dusted boulders. Both the generators and their
   output are committed (D-081), two builds are byte-identical, and a missing
   `generated/` costs cover rather than costing the client.

2. **Placement is a pure static class**, `ground_cover.gd`, shaped exactly like
   `ResourceVisuals` (D-087) — biome, the six neighbour biomes, moisture,
   elevation and the cell index in; a list of (model, in-cell offset, yaw,
   scale) out. Per-biome palettes and densities are DATA TABLES, not branches.
   Rolls are FNV over (cell, salt, prop index), one stream per decision, so a
   cell's dressing is a pure function of the cell and every client dresses the
   map identically without anything being sent.

3. **Ground cover is the deliberate opposite of a resource node**, and the
   contrast is the design:

   | | Resource nodes (D-087) | Ground cover |
   |---|---|---|
   | Owned by | the server; simulation entities | the client; cosmetic only |
   | Fog | gated, with felling events on the wire | not gated — a grass tuft leaks nothing |
   | Wire cost | reveal and depletion events | zero |
   | Determinism | server-authoritative | a pure hash of the cell |

   Nothing about cover is replicated, and nothing about it should become
   replicated. It is stated here, in `ground_cover.gd`'s header and in
   `art/scatter/props.py`'s, because the one way this feature goes wrong later
   is somebody "fixing" it by putting it on the wire.

4. **Two gameplay rules constrain the art itself.** Props stay SHORT —
   `art/scatter/props.py` FAILS THE BUILD above 0.5 units authored, and
   `GroundCover.MAX_PROP_HEIGHT` bounds authored height × `SCALE_MAX` at 0.58
   against a ~1.8-unit soldier — and a cell already holding a resource node, a
   building or a wall gets none. The occupancy answer is an INPUT
   (`props_for(..., occupied)`), supplied by the caller: a pure module has no
   business reaching into simulation state. Unit readability is tactical
   information, the same reason D-045's render LOD draws a distant squad
   thinner and never smaller.

5. **Density is a shipped tunable that something reads.** Per-biome base
   densities swing with moisture and thin smoothly with elevation (exposed
   high ground is barer), and `GroundCover.density_scale` scales the lot for a
   future quality setting. Measured on the shipped Standard map (84×96, seed
   1337, shipped thresholds): **21,303 props over 6,380 vegetated cells —
   3.34 per cell**, zero on water and deep water, forest 4.5/cell, grassland
   3.5, beach 2.6, dry grassland 1.8, mountain 2.3.

**Rationale:** The owner asked for foliage that makes a region read as a
place. Copying `ResourceVisuals`'s shape was not imitation for its own sake:
it is the split that keeps the interesting failure modes (cacti in a forest,
clutter on open water, a prop tall enough to hide a unit) testable headless,
with the client left holding only scene-tree plumbing. Deriving cover on the
client rather than placing it on the server is what makes it free — the
terrain is already generated identically on both sides, so the dressing costs
one hash per prop and nothing at all on the wire.

**Rejected alternatives:** Fog-gating cover (it would mean sending it, to
hide something that reveals nothing — the whole point of the contrast with
D-087); server-placed props (simulation cost and wire cost for decoration);
a dense grass carpet (unit readability, and a per-frame cost nobody budgeted);
per-prop scene nodes (the M4 `by_id` shape at 20k+ instances — batching is one
MultiMesh per (16-cell chunk, model), with the torus tax per chunk, D-035);
vertex-coloured props like every other authored model (a MultiMesh overrides
`COLOR`, which is how every soldier came out black in M7 — props carry real
glTF materials instead, the same path the authored trees already render
through); rolling dice in the art generator for prop variety (a seed is a
hidden input D-081's byte-identical rule forbids — variety comes from the
per-cell yaw/scale hash instead).

**Consequences, and one defect found on the way:** the glTF material path is
NOT colour-neutral. Godot's importer converts `baseColorFactor` linear → sRGB
and nothing converts it back, so an authored 0.36 arrived as albedo 0.63 and
every fern rendered frosted beside ground painted with the same numbers
through the vertex-colour path. `art/lib/bake.py` pre-compensates, the
authored colours are recorded in the manifest, and a test asserts the imported
`StandardMaterial3D` matches what was painted. This is the fourth member of
the family that includes the VAT's silent VRAM compression: **a value that
crosses an asset pipeline is not the value that comes out the other side
unless something checks.**

`just gen-cover-preview` is the new recipe and the primary evidence — cover on
generated terrain with a real squad standing in it, so "cover never hides a
unit" is looked at rather than asserted.

**Revisit trigger:** if `bench-render` at 1,000 squads shows cover costing more
than the ~4 ms the plan budgeted for it, density comes down before the budget
goes up (`density_scale` exists for exactly that). If cover ever acquires
gameplay meaning — concealment, movement cost, flammability — that is a new
decision and a new mechanism, because every claim above depends on it having
none. If a future map preset produces PEAK cells in quantity (the shipped
Standard map at its default seed has none, and only ~67 MOUNTAIN cells), the
high-ground palettes get their first real look.
